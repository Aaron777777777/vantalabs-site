Separate chores that have been marked done from chores you have actually checked and accepted.

Review the job before it becomes progress

Children may genuinely believe a task is finished when part of it has been missed. A quick parent review helps avoid confusion and gives you a natural point to explain what still needs doing.

Once you are happy with the result, you can approve the chore and confirm any progress attached to it. Until then, it remains submitted rather than fully accepted.

That distinction can make the routine easier to explain to children too. Marking a chore complete means it is ready for you to look at; approval means you have checked it and accepted the result. The two steps stay separate without turning an everyday household job into a lengthy process.

Submitted chores remain pending. Progress is confirmed only after the parent has reviewed and approved the work.

Quick questions

Why is approval protected by the Parent PIN?

The Parent PIN keeps chore approval and other protected parent actions on the adult side of the shared device rather than inside the child-facing routine.

Can a child approve a chore they have just completed?

No. A child can mark the chore ready for review, but the parent or guardian keeps the final approval decision.

How much work does approving a completed chore involve?

Usually very little. The approval step is intended to be a quick parent review before the task and any related progress are confirmed.
