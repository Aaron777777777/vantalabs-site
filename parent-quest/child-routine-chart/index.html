Give the child a routine of their own

A personal chart keeps the child focused on what belongs to them. It can include regular chores, preparation tasks and other family responsibilities.

The parent controls the routine and can adapt it as the child grows or becomes more independent.

Because the chart belongs to one child profile, it can stay focused on the steps that matter to that child. Parents can simplify it during busy periods, add new responsibilities later and keep the routine separate from a sibling who may need something completely different.

Build the chart around the child. Parents should consider age, ability, school demands and the amount of support the child needs.

Quick questions

Can a child routine chart change as the child gets older?

Yes. Parents can edit the child’s routine as responsibilities, school schedules and levels of independence change.

Do tasks on a child routine chart always need rewards?

No. The parent decides whether any particular task should contribute towards reward progress or simply remain part of the child’s normal routine.

Does the chart replace a parent checking what the child is doing?

No. It gives the child a clearer routine to follow, but the parent still decides what needs checking, explaining or supervising.
