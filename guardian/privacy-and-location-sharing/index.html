Family location deserves careful handling

Most families looking into family privacy and location sharing are not trying to watch a phone from morning until night. They usually want a calmer answer to a practical question: keeping access limited, transparent and proportionate. Guardian is designed around that idea. A connected child phone shares its latest available location inside a private family account, while Home and School safe zones can provide arrival and leaving notifications during ordinary journeys.

When Guardian is being used for privacy and location sharing, phone-location readings still have practical limits. GPS can drift around buildings, mobile signal can weaken, and permissions, battery-saving settings or operating-system restrictions can delay background updates. The information is therefore best treated as useful family context rather than exact proof of somebody’s position or timing. Notification delivery can also be affected by device and network conditions, while Guardian’s SOS feature gives a connected child device another way to alert trusted adults rather than replacing emergency services.

Guardian is not an emergency service. Location and notifications can be affected by GPS, connectivity, permissions, battery settings and operating-system restrictions.

Is Guardian available on iPhone and Android for privacy and location sharing?

What can affect Guardian location information used for privacy and location sharing?

GPS, signal, buildings, permissions, battery settings and operating-system restrictions can affect the phone-location context involved in privacy and location sharing.

No. Guardian can provide useful family context around privacy and location sharing, but it is not an emergency service. In immediate danger, contact the emergency services directly.
