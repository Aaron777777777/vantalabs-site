Family SOS alerts for urgent situations

Guardian provides families with an SOS alert route from a connected child device to trusted adults in the private Guardian family. It is designed to help the family recognise that the child is asking for attention and coordinate a response.

An extra family SOS option, not an emergency service

A connected child can use Guardian's SOS feature to ask trusted adults in their private Guardian family for urgent attention. An alert may include the latest available phone-location and device information, helping family members decide what to do next. The reported location can be old or inaccurate, so it should never be treated as proof of where someone is now.

Agree a family response plan in advance

  1. Choose trusted adults: Agree who should respond to a Guardian SOS alert and who can contact the child directly.
  2. Check the devices: Keep the child phone charged and connected where possible, and review notification permissions on the adults' phones.
  3. Discuss what SOS means: Explain when your child should use it and what to do if a message or notification cannot be sent.
  4. Plan an alternative: Agree who your child should call or approach if their device has no signal, no battery or no response.

What should parents do after an SOS?

Try to contact the child directly, assess whatever information is available and coordinate with the other trusted adults. Do not wait for Guardian to refresh if there may be an immediate danger: contact emergency services directly. Notifications and location reporting can be delayed by connectivity, permissions, battery settings and operating-system restrictions.

Quick questions

Who can receive family SOS information in Guardian?

SOS information stays within the private Guardian family for trusted adults with access rather than being placed on a public profile.

Can the SOS alert help family adults decide who should respond?

Yes. Available location and device context can provide useful information while trusted adults coordinate who should contact the child or take further action.

Should a family rely only on Guardian for an emergency response?

No. Notification delivery cannot be guaranteed, and Guardian is not an emergency service or a substitute for direct emergency contact.
