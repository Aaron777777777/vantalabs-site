GPS tracker for children

A GPS tracker for children that respects family privacy

A GPS tracker for children that runs on a phone depends on that phone being able to obtain and share location information. Guardian can show the latest location the connected device has reported and use that information for features such as Home and School alerts.

Set realistic expectations for phone GPS

Parents should expect normal variation. Indoor environments, large buildings, weak coverage, location permissions, background restrictions and battery-saving behaviour can all affect how accurately or frequently a phone reports.

The technology also works better when children understand why it is being used. Families can agree when location will be checked, which trusted adults have access and when direct communication is still expected.

Guardian cannot turn a phone into a perfectly continuous tracker or guarantee a child’s safety. It provides location and alert context for families and is not an emergency service.

Quick questions

Can a child phone provide perfectly continuous GPS tracking?

No. Phone reporting depends on GPS, connectivity, permissions, background activity and battery conditions, so gaps and delays can occur.

What settings matter for a phone-based GPS tracker for children?

Location permissions, background access and battery restrictions can all influence whether the connected phone is able to keep reporting normally.

Should GPS tracking replace ordinary contact with a child?

No. Guardian can provide useful family context, but direct communication and agreed family routines remain important.
