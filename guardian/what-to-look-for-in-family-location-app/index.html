Choose a family location app by behaviour, not hype

Look for useful context around the location itself. Battery status, update timing and clear explanations of GPS limitations can be more helpful than a map that simply appears to promise constant precision.

Place-based alerts are also worth considering because they can answer routine questions without encouraging constant monitoring. Families may also want to know whether adult location sharing is optional and how trusted-adult access is managed.

Finally, separate location tools from safety guarantees. No family-location app can prove somebody is safe simply because a phone appears on a map, and Guardian is not an emergency service.

Quick questions

What privacy information should a family location app make clear?

Families should be able to understand who has access to location information, how trusted adults are managed and whether adult sharing is optional.

Why should an app show more than just a map position?

Battery and update context can help families understand whether the connected phone has reported recently and whether an older position needs cautious interpretation.

Should a family location app be treated as a safety guarantee?

No. Phone location is useful context, but it cannot prove that somebody is safe or replace direct communication and emergency action when needed.
