Family location data should stay carefully controlled

Family location data can reveal more than a point on a map. Home, School, regular journeys and frequently visited places can all say something about a family’s routine, so Guardian keeps child-device information within the parent-managed family rather than publishing it through public profiles or social discovery.

Family location data deserves careful handling

Parents should still decide carefully which trusted adults need access and review that access when circumstances change. Protecting the phones and accounts used by the family is part of protecting the location information itself.

Location data also needs to be interpreted sensibly. GPS conditions, permissions, connectivity and battery restrictions can affect what the connected child phone reports, so a map position should be treated as useful device context rather than proof of somebody’s exact physical position.

Clear family conversations matter too. Children should understand why location sharing is being used and who can see the available information. Guardian supports private family coordination, but it is not an emergency service.

Quick questions

Why should families treat location data carefully?

Location information can reveal places such as Home and School as well as everyday routines, so access should be limited to trusted adults who genuinely need it.

Is child location information published through a public Guardian profile?

No. Guardian keeps connected child-device information within the private, parent-managed family rather than a public social profile.

What should parents do when a trusted adult no longer needs access?

Review the family access and remove people who no longer need the child-device information, particularly when family or caregiving arrangements change.
