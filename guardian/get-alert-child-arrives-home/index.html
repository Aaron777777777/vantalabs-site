A Home arrival alert can replace the usual check-in message

Most families looking into Home arrival alerts are not trying to watch a phone from morning until night. They usually want a calmer answer to a practical question: knowing the child made it back safely. Guardian is designed around that idea. A connected child phone shares its latest available location inside a private family account, while Home and School safe zones can provide arrival and leaving notifications during ordinary journeys.

When Guardian is being used for home-arrival alerts, phone-location readings still have practical limits. GPS can drift around buildings, mobile signal can weaken, and permissions, battery-saving settings or operating-system restrictions can delay background updates. The information is therefore best treated as useful family context rather than exact proof of somebody’s position or timing. Notification delivery can also be affected by device and network conditions, while Guardian’s SOS feature gives a connected child device another way to alert trusted adults rather than replacing emergency services.

Guardian is not an emergency service. Location and notifications can be affected by GPS, connectivity, permissions, battery settings and operating-system restrictions.

Quick questions

Is Guardian available on iPhone and Android for home-arrival alerts?

Yes. Guardian is available through the App Store on iPhone and Google Play on Android, including the Guardian features relevant to home-arrival alerts.

What can affect Guardian location information used for home-arrival alerts?

GPS, signal, buildings, permissions, battery settings and operating-system restrictions can affect the phone-location context involved in home-arrival alerts.

Can Guardian replace emergency services when using it for home-arrival alerts?

No. Guardian can provide useful family context around home-arrival alerts, but it is not an emergency service. In immediate danger, contact the emergency services directly.
