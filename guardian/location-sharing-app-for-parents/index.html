Location sharing for parents who want useful updates

Guardian lets parents use child-device location features without turning adult location sharing into a requirement. A trusted adult can take part in the private family while deciding separately whether their own position should be shared.

Location sharing that remains optional for adults

Authorised child-device information can give parents practical context through the Family Map, device status and familiar Home or School alerts. The goal is useful coordination rather than compulsory two-way tracking between adults.

Parents can therefore choose different sharing boundaries for children and adults. What matters is that everyone involved understands how the family has chosen to use the app and who has access to the available information.

Location shown from a phone can still be affected by GPS conditions, connectivity, permissions and battery settings. Guardian does not provide guaranteed or perfectly accurate tracking and is not an emergency service.

Quick questions

Does a parent have to share their own location in Guardian?

No. Adult location sharing is optional and can be left off independently of authorised child-device location features.

Can parents rely on alerts instead of repeatedly checking a child’s map?

Home and School alerts can provide familiar journey milestones, while the Family Map remains available when extra context is genuinely needed.

Can parent location information ever be delayed or inaccurate?

Yes. Like other phone-based location data, it can be affected by GPS conditions, signal, permissions and battery behaviour.
