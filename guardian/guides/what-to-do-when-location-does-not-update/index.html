A missing location update usually has a practical explanation

Most families looking into location updates that stop are not trying to watch a phone from morning until night. They usually want a calmer answer to a practical question: checking battery, signal, permissions and background restrictions. Guardian is designed around that idea. A connected child phone shares its latest available location inside a private family account, while Home and School safe zones can provide arrival and leaving notifications during ordinary journeys.

When Guardian is being used for location-update troubleshooting, phone-location readings still have practical limits. GPS can drift around buildings, mobile signal can weaken, and permissions, battery-saving settings or operating-system restrictions can delay background updates. The information is therefore best treated as useful family context rather than exact proof of somebody’s position or timing. Notification delivery can also be affected by device and network conditions, while Guardian’s SOS feature gives a connected child device another way to alert trusted adults rather than replacing emergency services.

Guardian is not an emergency service. Location and notifications can be affected by GPS, connectivity, permissions, battery settings and operating-system restrictions.
