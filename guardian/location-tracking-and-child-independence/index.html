Location sharing that leaves room for growing independence

Location sharing does not have to mean checking every movement. As children become more independent, families can use Guardian around agreed routines and the moments where extra context is genuinely useful.

A calmer way to stay informed

Home and School alerts can provide familiar milestones without requiring a parent to keep the Family Map open throughout the journey. When something differs from the usual routine, the available map, battery and update information can provide additional context.

The balance can change as a child grows. Families can review when location is checked, which trusted adults have access and when direct communication is more appropriate than opening the app.

Phone location still has normal limits, so Guardian cannot guarantee exact positions, notification delivery or safety. Used openly and proportionately, it can support independence rather than replace trust; Guardian is not an emergency service.

Quick questions

Can family location sharing still leave room for child independence?

Yes. Families can use routine alerts and occasional map checks rather than treating location sharing as a reason to monitor every movement.

Should location-sharing rules stay the same as a child gets older?

Not necessarily. Families can review when the app is checked, who has access and how much location context is appropriate as independence grows.

Does Guardian guarantee a child’s exact position while they are out independently?

No. Guardian relies on phone location reporting, which can be affected by GPS, connectivity, permissions, battery conditions and device restrictions.
