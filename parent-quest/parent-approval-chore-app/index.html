Keep the final decision with the parent

A completion button is useful because it tells you a job is ready to review. It should not automatically decide that the task was finished properly or that a reward has been earned.

Parent Quest adds a quick parent check between those two steps. You can review the chore, approve it when you are satisfied and keep the outcome consistent with the rules in your own home.

That keeps the child involved without handing over the decision that affects progress or rewards. Children can show that they believe the work is finished, while the parent still decides whether the chore meets the expectation that was originally set.

Completed does not mean approved. The child can submit the chore, but the parent confirms whether it should count.

Quick questions

What does the Parent PIN protect in the chore approval flow?

It protects approval and other parent-only actions so children can use their part of the routine without controlling the final result.

Can children award themselves approval after finishing a chore?

No. They can mark the chore complete, but approval remains with the parent or guardian.

Is parent approval meant to slow the chore routine down?

No. The idea is a quick check between completion and confirmed progress, not a complicated inspection process.
