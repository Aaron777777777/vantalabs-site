Use the child phone for useful family location updates

Guardian uses the child’s connected phone for family location features rather than requiring a separate dedicated tracker. Trusted adults in the private family can check the latest available phone-location context when they need it.

Device context matters as much as the map

Battery status and last-update information help parents understand the device behind the map. A low battery or an older timestamp can explain why a position has not changed even when the child’s journey has continued.

Phone conditions matter because location reporting depends on connectivity, permissions and background activity. If those are restricted, Guardian may receive fewer or later updates from the child device.

A phone tracker should be treated as one source of family context rather than an exact or guaranteed record of movement. Guardian supports communication and is not an emergency service.

Guardian is not an emergency service. Location and notifications can be affected by GPS, connectivity, permissions, battery settings and operating-system restrictions.

Quick questions

Does Guardian require a separate GPS tracker for a child?

Guardian uses location information from the connected child phone for its family location features rather than requiring a separate dedicated tracker.

Why is the phone’s last-update time useful?

It helps parents see whether the map position is based on a recent report or whether the device has not updated for a while.

Can another phone setting stop normal Guardian location reporting?

Permissions, background restrictions, connectivity and battery-saving behaviour can all affect whether the connected phone continues reporting normally.
